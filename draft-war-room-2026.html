How to think about this draft

The plan

Round-by-round, by draft slot

Three scripts for a 10-team snake. The shared spine: hammer RB and WR through round 5, take one shot at Bowers/McBride if the board offers it, land a QB in rounds 6–9, and spend every late pick on upside — rookies, handcuffs, and the sleeper list above. Kicker and D/ST go in the last two rounds. Always.

Picks 1–3 · The anchor slot

You get a true difference-maker; your R2 pick comes at the wheel

R1
Bijan, Gibbs, or Chase. All three are correct; Gibbs has the cleanest 2026 setup with Montgomery gone.
R2
At pick 18–20: Bowers/McBride if one is there, else best WR standing (Nabers, Collins).
R3–5
Two more RB/WR — target Chase Brown, McConkey, Loveland as they slide.
R6–9
QB from tier 2–3 (Lamar is the priority), plus FLEX depth.
R10+
Sleepers and handcuffs: Diggs, Downs, Okonkwo, your RB1's backup.

Picks 4–7 · The minefield

The CMC/Achane zone — discipline here wins the draft

R1
Prefer Taylor or Jefferson over McCaffrey/Achane at the same cost — same ceiling, fewer ways to zero.
R2
Pair the opener: RB1 → elite WR (ARSB, JSN); WR1 → Jeanty/Hampton.
R3–5
Leave with 3 RB/WR starters minimum. Allen occasionally slips to R3 here — take him.
R6–9
QB if you passed on Allen; add a high-floor WR (Higgins, G. Wilson types linger).
R10+
Upside only. A bench of safe veterans wins nothing.

Picks 8–10 · The turn

Back-to-back picks — draft in pairs, plan two tiers at once

R1–2
Two from the 8–13 shelf: Jeanty + ARSB, or Lamb + Saquon if his age discount appeals. Double RB is live here too.
R3–4
Next turn: McBride often survives to ~22 — pair him with a tier-3 WR.
R5–6
Jeremiyah Love tends to land right at this turn. So does LaPorta if you skipped TE.
R7–9
QB + third RB. At the turn you can start a positional run instead of chasing one.
R10+
Pairs of darts: rookie WR + handcuff each turn.

Logistics

ESPN room notes & draft-day checklist

Know your ESPN room

  • Confirm the scoring before anything else. ESPN defaults to full PPR — half-PPR or standard changes the whole top of this board (RBs and TD-dependent players rise).
  • ESPN's draft-room ranks are ESPN's, not consensus. Casual rooms follow the default list, so players ESPN is low on (check the values table) fall further than this board suggests — let them come to you.
  • Pre-rank your queue. Load ~20 favorites into the player queue before the draft; if your connection drops, autopick drafts from your queue, not ESPN's list.
  • Mock in the actual lobby. Two or three ESPN mock drafts from your real slot beats any article — including this one.
  • Roster math: 9 starters, 7 bench. With one of each K/D/ST that leaves 14 picks for skill players — roughly 5 RB, 5–6 WR, 1–2 QB, 1–2 TE.
  • Playoffs are weeks 15–17. A cupcake late-season schedule is a real tiebreaker between similar players.

The last 24 hours

  • Re-check ADP the morning of the draft — August camp news moves players a full round in days. This board is a mid-August snapshot.
  • Scrub the injury report. Verify Kittle (Achilles), LaPorta (back), Taylor's QB situation, and any preseason knocks before trusting a price here.
  • Write down your tier cutoffs for RB and WR — when a tier is one player deep on the clock, take it.
  • Never draft K or D/ST before the last two rounds, even when a run starts. Especially when a run starts.
  • Draft one handcuff — your own RB1's backup, not a stranger's.
  • Leave with upside. The last four picks should all be players with a story to become weekly starters by October.
